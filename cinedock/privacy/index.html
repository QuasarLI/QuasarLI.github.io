CineDock / development notes

Privacy starts with an accurate data map.

Reviewed October 29, 2026. CineDock remains in private development; there is no public customer release.

Not a finalized release privacy policy: these notes describe the reviewed development implementation. Publisher identity, the app-specific privacy contact, retention decisions, and final user-facing deletion instructions still require owner approval. Do not treat this page as evidence of Google OAuth verification or a completed compliance review.

This website is separate from the desktop app

This site does not connect your Google account or receive your Drive library. Its static hosting and session-only theme setting are described in the Quasar Labs website privacy notice. Those website statements are not a claim that the desktop app has no network access or stores no data.

Google Drive access under development

When you choose to connect Drive in the reviewed desktop implementation, CineDock opens the system browser for Google authorization. It requests the restricted https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.readonly scope: read-only permission to view and download files throughout your authorized Drive. This is not permission limited to selected videos or files created by CineDock. It does not request Drive write or delete permission.

The app reads account identifiers, display name, and email address to identify the connected account. Library browsing reads file and folder identifiers, names, MIME types, sizes, modification times, resource keys, shortcut information, and capability flags. Playback downloads media bytes; it is not metadata-only access.

The reviewed Drive path communicates directly with Google over HTTPS and feeds the local player through an on-device loopback media bridge. No CineDock-operated off-device media relay was identified in that path. This does not establish a product-wide no-sharing guarantee for optional remote-play features, other integrations, or diagnostics.

Real-account library browsing and media playback acceptance remain open. A browser authorization callback is not proof of complete Drive integration or Google's approval.

What the reviewed implementation keeps locally

Saved refresh grants are protected locally with Windows DPAPI. Local playback history includes account and file identifiers, playback position, and duration. That protection for saved grants is not a claim that all local history, media, or temporary files are encrypted.

Playback uses authenticated range requests and local player buffering. No global cache-retention limit has been verified. Optional download staging also uses local temporary files and recovery metadata; final retention and cleanup instructions are not yet settled.

A separately confirmed download saves a permanent original to the local destination you choose. Optional saved-HFR conversion can create another local output after a separate action. Ordinary playback does not require that conversion. Completed originals and derived outputs are your retained files, not a temporary streaming cache that disconnecting automatically removes.

Access revocation is not file deletion

The development service has a disconnect operation that can remove local saved credentials and attempt Google revocation. These are separate outcomes: an offline or failed revocation request is not confirmation that Google has revoked access. End-to-end live revocation and deletion have not been verified.

You can review or remove the app's account access through Google Account connections. Removing access does not erase previously saved local files or history. The reviewed implementation has a playback-history clearing path, but the final user-facing procedure and its relationship to disconnect still need confirmation. Do not assume disconnect clears history.

Completed downloads and converted outputs remain until you manage those files separately. Disconnect also does not promise deletion of an externally supplied client-configuration file, all temporary or recovery files, backups, or copies on other devices. No automatic secure-erasure or immediate-cleanup guarantee is made.

Using a separate Google browser session or Drive for desktop is a user-managed local-file workflow, not an alternative grant of native Drive API access. Revoking CineDock access does not sign out those separate clients or delete their files and caches. Their sync behavior and cloud-deletion effects are governed by those clients, not CineDock's disconnect operation.

There is not yet a confirmed CineDock-specific privacy or deletion-request contact. Use the existing general Quasar Labs inquiry route for release questions, without sending credentials or private media. That route is not a published deletion-service commitment.

What must be settled before a public Google-connected release

The owner must approve the responsible publisher identity, a monitored support/privacy/deletion contact, the final feature and processor inventory, retention periods, and usable token/history/temporary-file deletion instructions. The release policy must accurately address data use, transfers, processors, retention, deletion, and the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. These notes do not substitute for that approved policy.

Production domain ownership, matching homepage/privacy/terms pages, production Desktop-client configuration, scope justification, and Google's restricted-scope review and any required assessment are separate steps. Neither local-only playback architecture nor a published product page establishes approval or an assessment exemption.
